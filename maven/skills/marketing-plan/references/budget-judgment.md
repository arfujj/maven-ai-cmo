# Budget judgment

Load when the plan includes money. These are defaults from running small budgets, not
laws. Deviate when the business in front of you argues for it, and say why in one line.
Perishable numbers (cost per click, Local Services Ads eligibility by trade, platform
minimums) are never written here; look them up at use time and date them in the plan.

## The order money goes in

1. **Count first.** If the business can't say how many calls or booked jobs came from
   marketing last month, the first dollars and hours go to counting: call tracking or a
   plain call log, form notifications that land somewhere, and conversion tracking
   that counts calls (`/google-ads-tracking`). Spending without a count means the plan
   can't keep or kill anything.
2. **Fix the leaks.** Missed calls (`/speed-to-lead`), a first screen that hides the
   number (`/first-screen-check`), a thin or stale review count (`/reviews`). These cost
   hours more than money and they raise the return on every dollar after them. A business
   that misses one call in four turns $1,000 of ads into $750 of chances.
3. **Win the free shelf.** The Google Business Profile and the map pack
   (`/google-business-profile`), then the pages that earn the plain results
   (`/local-seo-plan`). Slow, compounding and cheap.
4. **Buy demand closest to intent.** For an urgent trade, someone typing "emergency
   plumber" is the cheapest good lead there is: search ads and, where the trade is
   eligible, Local Services Ads. Meta and display create demand; they work better for
   planned, visual or high-ticket work (remodels, roofing replacement, med spa, implants)
   and for retargeting people who already visited.
5. **Hold back a test slice**, roughly 10 to 20 percent, for one new thing at a time
   with a written keep or kill rule.

## Small budgets (under about $1,500 a month in ads)

- **One channel, one campaign.** Splitting a small budget across cities, services or
  platforms starves every piece of the data it needs to learn. Run one broad campaign
  on the best-intent channel first; split only once it spends its budget every day and
  the business can see which part pays. `/paid-ads` carries the full reasoning.
- **No brand-new channel before the leaks close.** Ten more leads into a site that gets
  no calls is the most common waste in local marketing.
- **Retargeting can wait** until the site gets enough visits to fill an audience.

## Matching the trade

| Kind of work | Where the money usually works first |
|---|---|
| Urgent (burst pipe, no heat, locked out, water damage, towing) | Search ads, Local Services Ads where eligible, the map pack, answering every call |
| Planned and local (HVAC replacement, roofing, remodel, landscaping) | Reviews and proof, the map pack, search ads in season, follow-up on every quote |
| Considered and personal (dental, med spa, legal, therapy) | Reviews, a page that answers fear and price questions, search, then Meta for visual work |
| Recurring service (lawn care, pest, pool, cleaning) | Reviews, door-to-door and neighbor referral offers, a strong first-visit offer, email to past customers |

## Agency view

For an agency planning a client's quarter, the scarce resource is usually team hours,
not ad money. Budget the retainer's hours against the bets the same way, and protect the
monthly report and the counting work, because those are what keep the client.

## Writing it into the plan

Show the split as a small table: line, monthly amount, what it buys, the keep or kill
rule. Totals must equal what the owner said, never more. If the stated budget can't fund
the plan, cut a bet and say which one waits, instead of quietly assuming more money.
