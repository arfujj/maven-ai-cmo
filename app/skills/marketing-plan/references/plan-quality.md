# Plan quality bar

Answer each question silently before handing over the plan. Fix every no; never show this
list to the user.

1. Is there exactly one number, with a baseline that is measured or honestly marked
   unknown?
2. Is the target a range with its math shown, built from the bets rather than hoped for?
3. Does the diagnosis name one leaking stage, with evidence a skeptic could check?
4. Are there three to five bets, each with a Maven skill, a leading indicator and a keep
   or kill rule written before launch?
5. Does the first bet fix the most downstream leak, or does the plan say why not?
6. Is there one result the owner can see inside the first two weeks?
7. Does "Not doing" name at least two tempting moves and why they lost?
8. Does the budget total equal what the owner stated or less, and do the owner hours fit
   what they said they have?
9. Is each bet placed with the season in mind (built before the rush, harvested in it)?
10. Are owner decisions three or fewer, each a yes or no with a date?
11. Could an owner read the plan in five minutes without a word of jargon? Replace
    "funnel", "CAC", "SERP", "attribution" and "omnichannel" with what they mean.
12. Does every estimated number carry `~`, and does the Sources and assumptions note say
    what rests on live data, local documents or general knowledge?

## What a weak plan looks like (and the fix)

- Ten priorities of equal weight. Fix: rank, keep five, move the rest to Not doing.
- "Post on social media three times a week" with no tie to the number. Fix: cut it or
  show the path from posts to booked jobs.
- A forecast pulled from industry averages. Fix: the business's own count, or unknown.
- Ads first while a third of calls go unanswered. Fix: re-order, leak first.
- A plan that needs the owner for ten hours a week. Fix: cut a bet or move work to Maven
  drafts with one approval step.
