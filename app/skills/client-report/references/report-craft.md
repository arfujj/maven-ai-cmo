# Report craft

Load before writing a client report. The difference between a report that keeps a client
and one that loses them is rarely the results. It's whether the owner believes it.

## The owner's three questions

1. Is it working? (The headline answers this, in the first sentence.)
2. What did I pay for? (What we did, in their words.)
3. What happens next, and what do you need from me? (Next month and the asks.)

Anything that answers none of these goes in the notes file, not the report.

## Calibration

**A 3/10 headline:** "October saw strong performance across channels, with impressions up
38% and engagement trending positively." It answers nothing, names no number the owner
counts, and leads with a vanity metric.

**An 8/10 headline:** "41 booked jobs came from marketing in October, up from 33 in
September and ahead of the 38 the plan called for. Most of the gain came from answering
missed calls." It names the owner's own count, the comparison, the plan, and the cause.
(Illustrative numbers.)

**A 3/10 credit line:** "Google Ads drove 62 conversions." The platform's claim, stated
as fact, often counting the same caller twice.

**An 8/10 credit line:** "Google Ads reports 62 conversions; your phone log shows 29 new
callers who said they found you on Google. We use the 29. A pause test in January would
settle how many of those would have called anyway."

## Down months

Say it first, give the cause if known, show what is being changed, and separate what the
agency controls from what it doesn't (weather, a competitor's price cut, a slow season
the plan predicted). Never bury a down month under a good leading indicator.

## Words to swap

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| impressions, reach | how many people saw it, only if it matters |
| CTR | how many clicked out of every hundred who saw it |
| conversions | calls, forms, booked jobs: name which |
| ROAS | dollars of jobs for each ad dollar, and say how it was counted |
| SERP, rankings | where you show up when someone searches "<trade> <town>" |
| optimized | say what changed |
| leveraged, synergy, holistic | cut |

## Length and form

A phone screen and three minutes. Short paragraphs, one small table at most (the number
by month), no charts the owner has to interpret. If the agency's template has a cover
page or logos, the agency adds them; the report text works pasted into an email.
